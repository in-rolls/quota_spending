# Chaturvedi, Das & Mahajan
versions: SSRN WP 3962068, Sep 2023 (77 pp; main text pp. 1–34, appendix pp. 43–77) → EDCC 73(2), Jan 2025, pp. 749–779 (online apps. A–C not in PDF)
bottom_line: The published article is the September 2023 WP after copyediting. All five main tables match number for number, and the sample, specifications, outcomes, mechanism section, Muslim-share framing and conclusions are unchanged. The only differences are Chaturvedi's affiliation, a longer acknowledgments note, a dropped abstract/JEL block (absent from the PDF), updated citation years, the WP's "ATE" corrected to "local average treatment effect", and slightly revised open-defecation figures in one footnote.

## Unchanged (verified)
- Title is identical (WP p. 1; pub p. 749).
- Sample: about 25M rural UP households and about 59,000 GPs; the 2015 sarpanch election; outcome is toilet receipt in 2016–17 among eligible households without a toilet at the end of 2015–16; GP-level clustering; weights = eligible households per GP (WP pp. 5, 19; pub pp. 752, 763).
- Design: fuzzy RD on the rule-based female-quota assignment, with linear control functions and fixed bandwidths 0.100/0.075/0.050. The CCT bandwidth of 0.475 is reported but not used. Heterogeneity is estimated by difference-in-discontinuities (Grembi et al.), eqs. (1)–(8) identical (WP pp. 19, 23, 30; pub pp. 763, 766–767, 771–772).
- Table 1 (overall effect): .0133 (.0156), .0200 (.0186), .0285 (.0235); all insignificant; N 2,470,191 / 1,962,725 / 1,457,226 (WP p. 20; pub p. 764).
- Table 2 (SQUAT, Female × Muslim share): .125***, .133***, .336, .434**, .378** (WP p. 22; pub p. 766).
- Table 3 (Female reservation × Muslim share): .292** (.139), .403** (.164), .539*** (.200); main effect −.0204/−.0272/−.0357, insignificant (WP p. 24; pub p. 767).
- Table 4 (REDS complaints): interaction for Muslim households .242** and .266*; for Hindu households −.0473 and −.100 (n.s.) (WP p. 29; pub p. 772).
- Table 5 (female-headed households): Muslim households .305***, .345***, .326**; Hindu households .0591, .0619, .0368 (n.s.) (WP p. 32; pub p. 773).
- Text CIs and magnitudes match: 13–28% average effect, +3–5 pp per 10 pp of Muslim share, ρ_I between 0.31 and 0.35, first stage 0.53–0.59, 98% of quota allotments predicted.
- Robustness and mechanism content is unchanged and appears in the same order: balance (C5), first stage (C6/C8), quadratic (C9), pre-existing characteristics (C11; only the primary-school outcome is significant), interactions with controls (C12), Muslim sarpanch (C13), delimitation IV (C14/App. B.1), NREGS placebo (C15/B.3), household-FE panel (C18/B.2), supply channel via close elections (B.4).
- The Muslim-share heterogeneity is framed the same way: a cultural purity norm narrows the Hindu gender gap; Muslim share proxies women's relative preference intensity; the demand channel is called "suggestive" in both versions. Caveat sentences match, e.g. "unable to rule out this possibility completely" (WP p. 27; pub p. 769).
- Conclusion text is identical apart from copyedits (WP p. 34; pub pp. 775–776).
- Appendix inventory: the WP has Tables C1–C22, Figs. B1 and C1–C10, and Apps. A–C, the same inventory the published article says is online.
- Table, figure, equation, footnote (1–18) and section numbering are the same; no renumbering beyond arabic → Roman section numerals.
## Caveats
- The comparison used a word-level diff of the extracted text of the full main body plus manual reading of every table. Equation typesetting garbled in extraction (e.g. "5" for "=", "2" for "−"), so equations were compared structurally, not glyph by glyph.
- The published online appendix (Tables C1–C22) was not available, so appendix coefficients (first stage, quadratic, delimitation, panel FE, etc.) could not be checked against the WP appendix.
- I did not check whether the journal's web page carries an abstract; I only know the PDF does not.
- No REVERSAL, MAGNITUDE or SAMPLE/OUTCOME differences were found.
