## Unchanged (verified)
- Title and abstract, word for word, including the headline "10 percentage point increase…2.1 percentage point reduction in neonatal mortality" (WP p.1 → p.164).
- Section structure I-VII with the same headings (WP pp.2-36 → pp.164-195).
- Sample and data: NFHS-2 1998-99 birth histories, non-migrant mothers; 76,732 constituency elections, 16 states, 1967-1998; more than 70,000 births to about 18,000 mothers in 246 districts; 1,826 district-years; median district has 9 seats; women's seat share mean 3.6% (sd 7.4%); 3.5% close-election margin; 7.45% of district-years have a close election (WP pp.9-12 → pp.169-172). Table 1A/1B/1C descriptives match number for number (N = 75,339; 8,853; 1,826; 1,558) (WP pdf pp.49-51 → pp.170-172).
- Design: mother fixed effects plus 2SLS with womenclose as the instrument; totalclose control; third-order vote-margin polynomial for each man-woman race; cohort FE; state trends; district-clustered SEs. Equations (1)-(4) are the same (WP pp.12-16, 31 → pp.173-177, 192).
- Table 2 (WP pdf p.52 → p.178): every coefficient, SE and star is the same. Neonatal OLS −0.0050/−0.0148/−0.0303; 2SLS −0.1176/−0.1942**/−0.2061*** (0.078); first-stage coefficient on womenclose about 0.89 (fn 13 → fn 12); first-stage F-statistics the same.
- Table 3 robustness, Table 4 lags/leads/placebos, Table 7 health-seeking (e.g. antenatal visits 3.6524**/2.7745*, iron 1.0663***/0.9518**, breastfeeding 1.2758***/1.3406**, F 124-167, N 7,448-8,429), Table 8 village goods (F 45.973): the numeric tokens match as multisets between versions, apart from OCR splits in the JSTOR text, which I checked by hand (WP pdf pp.53-54, 57-58 → pp.181, 183, 190, 193).
- Outcome set: neonatal and infant mortality (main); postneonatal, under-5 and to age 14 (appendix, insignificant); fertility and sex of births; antenatal care, iron, tetanus, breastfeeding, place of delivery; village health and non-health infrastructure. All of these appear in both versions.
- Mechanisms and extensions text: larger effects for first births, urban, low-caste and middle-wealth children; no difference by politician caste; no weakening after 1993; larger influence under a female chief minister; no same-party effect; footnote on no state-income effect (WP pp.23-34 → pp.184-193).
- Conclusions section: only copyedits (WP pp.34-36 → pp.193-195).
## Caveats
- I compared the two versions with an automated word-level diff of the body text, plus a check that each table has the same multiset of numeric tokens. The JSTOR OCR splits digits ("0.01 1 8"), and its layout scrambles column order in some descriptive tables. I resolved every numeric mismatch by reading the text, and I rendered published Table 5 to check the label error.
- I did not compare cell by cell for column alignment in Tables 3, 4, 7 and 8. I relied on matching multisets plus spot reads (e.g. Table 4: −0.2061*** at lag −1 and −0.1975** at lag 0 in both). A column swap with identical values would get past this check.
- The online appendix (Tables A1-A12, Data Appendix) is not in either PDF, so I could not compare it.
- Working-paper tables and figures sit at the end without printed folios, so I cite them by PDF page. Text pages are cited as printed pages (PDF − 1).
