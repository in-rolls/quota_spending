# Rajaraman & Gupta
versions: "Further Evidence on the Policy Impact of Randomised Political Reservation", NIPFP working paper (no date printed on p.1; PDF created 22 Jan 2009; 35 pp.) → "Public expenditure choices and gender quotas", Indian Growth and Development Review 5(2) 2012, pp.108-130 (Emerald copy: PDF page n = printed page n+106)
bottom_line: The data (2005-06 survey of 776/780 GPs in 17 districts of MP, Chhattisgarh, Rajasthan and Orissa) and the water-probit results are essentially unchanged. The headline null for a female sarpanch on water spending stays insignificant. The WP's one significant gender result, a positive female-reservation effect on spending on buildings (Table 4, *0.23), and the own-revenue analysis were cut from the published version. A formal model and OLS/quantum robustness checks were added, and the published paper's message became that fundamentals, not the head's gender, drive spending.

## Unchanged (verified)
- Survey: 17 districts (9 backward + 8 comparators) in MP, CH, RJ, OR; 78 blocks; 780 GPs out of 6,301 (12.38%); reference year 2005-06; 4 GPs missing SGRY spending, giving N=776 (WP pp10-11; pub pp114-115).
- Table 1 / Table I expenditure descriptives are identical: road share 59.92 (weighted), GPs with spending 668/351/428/325, percent 86.08/45.23/55.15/41.88 (WP p24; pub p115).
- Water probit, non-gender coefficients identical in Spec1/Spec2: per-capita SGRY ***0.0018/***0.0021; handpumps **−0.0158/**−0.0142; pop per village **−0.00004 (Spec1); pop density *−0.00005 (Spec2); LR χ² 44.90/42.26; pseudo-R² 0.0421/0.0396; correct prediction 58.76/59.02 (WP p25; pub p119).
- Disease probit columns kept from the WP match its coefficients exactly (e.g. ethnofract 0.4035/0.4256, handpumps −0.0160/−0.0124, ponds 0.0385/0.0384, pseudo-R² 0.2456/0.3911) (WP p27; pub p117).
- Balance tests (Table A4 → AIII) and sarpanch education (A5 → AIV) are identical: 255 reserved, 286 female sarpanches (WP pp34-35; pub p130).
- Core water conclusion is unchanged: the head's gender is not significant, and the one-third quota on members is offered as the likely reason spending lines up with fundamentals (WP pp16, 19; pub pp120-123).

## Caveats
- Neither version reports standard errors (the published Table II notes say they are "available from the authors"). Significance comparisons rest only on the stars.
- The WP is undated. "2009" comes from PDF metadata (created 22 Jan 2009) and the filename.
- The published 2.9× jump in the reservation coefficient reflects a change in what is reported (an aggregated marginal effect at the means plus a predictnl correction for interactions), not new data. Both versions are insignificant.
- WP Table 4 (buildings) does not say whether its entries are marginal effects. A 0.23 shift in probability is large, and the published paper never resolves the point.
- Page numbers come from text extraction (WP page footers; Emerald margin page numbers), not from rendered pages. Placement within a page is approximate to ±1 page for published text that crosses a page break.
- The prompt suggested the paper might be about Karnataka. It is not: both versions cover MP, Chhattisgarh, Rajasthan and Orissa.
