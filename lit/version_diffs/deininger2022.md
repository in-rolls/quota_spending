# Deininger, Jin, Nagarajan & Singh
versions: WPS 9350 (Aug 2020, "Political Reservation and Female Labor Force Participation in Rural India") → OKR author manuscript (23 pp, JDS title) → J. Development Studies 58(9) 2022, 1772–1790
bottom_line: The headline result holds in every version: current reservation has no effect on women's labour-force participation (LFP), and past reservation raises it, by +8.2 pp in the WPS and +8.9 pp in the JDS, mostly through NREGS. Between the WPS and the manuscript, though, every regression was re-estimated, and several secondary stars changed: the current-reservation education-decision effects disappear, the married-subsample SEs grow 25–45%, and some governance F-tests are recomputed. The manuscript's tables and text match the JDS (all 5 tables checked number by number). The only JDS-specific changes are dropping the Bonferroni sentence, the star legends, references, funding, and the removal of "agency" from the intro's outcome list.

Page conventions: WPS = printed page numbers (printed = pdf − 2). MS = pdf page (same as printed). JDS = printed journal page (pdf + 1771). Women's past effect = (Res. before) + (Res. before × fem), i.e. WPS α2+β2 = MS/JDS β2+β4.

## Unchanged (verified)
- Manuscript = JDS for all five tables. I diffed every number in MS Tables 1–5 against the JDS OCR: the only mismatches were OCR-dropped minus signs. I also rendered JDS Tables 2, 3, 4 and 5 (1781, 1783, 1785, 1786) and they match the MS digits and stars. Manuscript prose = JDS prose apart from the copyedits listed above (word-level diff).
- Sample frame: complete enumeration of 190 villages in "13 states", 2014/15 ARIS-REDS round, 275,677 individuals in 91,984 households, 23,350 job-card households, 66,362 working-age individuals (WPS 8–9; MS 7; JDS 1777). The "12 main states" wording (WPS 3; JDS 1774) and the footnote listing 12 states (WPS 8; JDS note 9) are inconsistent in every version, not just the JDS. All versions also name Gujarat among the decision-module states, though Gujarat is not a survey state (WPS 19, 23; JDS 1778, 1786).
- Identification: random rotation of women's reservation; R1 = reserved at the most recent election, R2 = reserved at the previous one (e.g. AP 2011/2006, Orissa 2012/2007); district FE; same control list; SEs clustered at village panchayat (WPS 11, 20; JDS 1779–80, note 12).
- Women's past effect on NREGS participation, 15 pp (0.150 in both); married women's net +9.1 pp and NREGS +17 pp (WPS 13–14; JDS 1783–84); wants-to-work-more +16 pp (0.163** in both); food decision 0.213*** → 0.204*** (still ***); nonfood past effect for men 0.145** → 0.143**.
- Governance: current effects on dated receipt (0.268***) and complaining (0.271***), and past effects on dated receipt (0.496***) and complaint addressed (0.241***), are identical to 3 dp (WPS 22; JDS 1785). The "no gender-specific governance effect; women lag on complaints" conclusion is the same (WPS 14; JDS 1784).
- The "half a standard deviation" and "most pronounced for married women" summary claims, and the Conclusion section, are word-for-word the same (WPS 3, 15; JDS 1774, 1785–86).

## Caveats
- The JDS is an image scan, so numbers were taken from OCR. Every JDS coefficient cited here was confirmed by rendering pages 1781, 1783, 1785 and 1786. JDS Table 1 (1778) was checked only by number-sequence match against the MS, not rendered.
- The online supplement (Tables A1–A6, the eq. 3–4 results, the sector regressions, any wage table, and the referenced Figure 1 map) was not available. The table above only says what was removed from the main text.
- Why WPS → MS estimates moved is not stated anywhere. The 540-person gender recode, the married/unmarried N change and the 25–45% SE growth in married-subsample regressions point to data cleaning or re-estimation; that is an inference. Some MS/JDS F-test p-values look implausible given their coefficients and SEs (women's "did complain" sum −0.103 with SEs ≈0.1 reported as p=0.000; the duplicate "β1+β2" rows in Table 4A). The WPS values for those tests look more internally consistent.
- Several WPS text claims don't match the WPS's own tables: married women's current effect "1.7 pp, marginally significant" against a table p of 0.272; the panel-A "6.3 and 2.2 points" whose column order is swapped. Some of the REVERSAL rows above are therefore text corrections rather than changes in estimates.
- The decision to treat single-star (p<0.10) crossings as REVERSAL follows the brief's definition. None of them touches the headline women's-past-reservation sums (β2+β4), which stay at p≤0.05 in every version. The interaction-only test β3+β4 for unmarried women does cross 5% (0.032 → 0.051).
